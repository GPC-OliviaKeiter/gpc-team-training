# Running the Engagement

Once a partner is Active, three recurring mechanisms carry the week: the
weekly call, the Monday written update, and, specifically for a Workflow
Assessment, the stakeholder interviews that produce the assessment itself.

## Running weekly calls

A consistent, time-boxed structure for the recurring partner call, so it
surfaces engineering progress, catches blockers and adoption gaps early,
recognizes wins, and closes with clear accountability. Total length is
typically 45-60 minutes, depending on how much time Client Topics and
Discovery need.

1. **Engineer updates (10 min).** What shipped this week, what's in
   progress, what's queued next. This front-loads value delivery and sets a
   positive tone.
2. **Blockers and adoption gaps (15 min), the main focus.** What's not
   being used and why, what's preventing implementation, where the client is
   stuck, what needs your attention to unblock them. This is where adoption
   problems get caught before they escalate, which is why it gets the
   largest block.
3. **Wins and adoption check (5 min).** What worked, what's getting team
   traction. Ends the heavy section on a positive note before moving into
   lighter agenda items.
4. **Client topics (5-10 min, only if flagged at the start).** Whatever the
   client raised in the opening, addressed with controlled timing so they
   feel heard without derailing the rest of the agenda.
5. **Discovery / expansion opportunities (5-10 min).** What's changed in
   their workflow, what's coming, where else they're feeling friction. This
   is the productized-service probe: see *Identifying & Pitching Upsell
   Opportunities* below for how to act on what surfaces here.
6. **Next steps and commitments (5 min).** Action items for GPC, action
   items for the client, confirm the next meeting.

Log every action item in ClickUp immediately after the call, split by owner
(GPC vs. client), so accountability tracks against a real task instead of
just meeting notes.

## Weekly client updates (every Monday)

Regular updates keep clients informed and confident even in a slow week.
Perceived forward motion is what keeps trust and retention intact.

1. Once the Project Manager posts the weekly report to the partner's ClickUp
   channel, review it and approve it. Verify technical accuracy and
   blockers with the engineer before it goes out.
2. Post it to the partner's chosen channel (Slack, email, or Teams).

**Tone:** confidence over apology. Even a quiet week gets a brief note: the
touchpoint itself is what matters, not the volume of news. Tag the full
channel if the client's team is five people or fewer; tag only the
responsible parties above that. Share **external** blockers only, never
internal capacity issues or team politics, and strip any internal ClickUp
links before sending.

Template shape: Completed Last Week / Blocked (external only) / Pending
Tasks / Action Needed. Keep it short, sharp, and outcomes-oriented. Detail
every task only if it's relevant to the client.

## Conducting stakeholder interviews (Workflow Assessment)

Interview volume is set by tier: **Silver** = 5 one-hour interviews, **Gold**
= 10 one-hour interviews.

1. **Align on stakeholders during kickoff.** Confirm the tier, then work with
   the client to name specific stakeholders. Aim for a spread across the
   departments most affected by the workflow being assessed, not just
   whoever's most available. Capture name, role, and contact info.
2. **Schedule.** Book each 1-hour slot, with a short description of the
   interview's purpose on the invite so stakeholders show up knowing what to
   expect. Track progress against the tier's total count.
3. **Run each interview to this shape**, adjusting pacing to the hour but
   keeping the sequence:
   1. **Set the agenda (2-3 min).** Time available, and the goal in plain
      terms: learn about them and their role, specifically repetitive or
      manual tasks that could be automated or better supported.
   2. **Background (3-5 min).** Tenure, team, role: who they report to and
      how their work fits the broader department.
   3. **Map the recurring work (10-15 min).** What they do daily, weekly,
      monthly. Push past the first answer to the full list.
   4. **Walk through it visually (15-20 min), the main focus.** Have them
      share their screen and walk through an actual report, spreadsheet, or
      tool. Ask where each number comes from, how many systems they're
      pulling from, what's manual versus automatic, where errors tend to
      slip in. A live walkthrough surfaces bottlenecks a verbal description
      won't.
   5. **Capture the wish list (5-10 min).** A running list of tedious or
      repetitive tasks they'd want help with, pushed beyond what's already
      surfaced.
   6. **Answer questions, stay on track.** Brief tool or process questions
      are fine; note anything that threatens to eat the remaining time for
      follow-up instead.
   7. **Close (2-3 min).** Recap that this is part of a broader round of
      conversations, and that recommendations come back after synthesis.
4. **File the recording immediately** into the designated ClickUp channel and
   tag findings.
5. **Repeat through the full tier count**, and flag an unresponsive or
   repeatedly-rescheduling stakeholder to the team early rather than letting
   it quietly block the timeline.

Findings synthesize against the Workflow Assessment Checklist to produce the
final recommendations. See engineering's build-requirements process, which
uses these interview findings as its primary input.
