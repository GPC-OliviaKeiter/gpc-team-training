# Onboarding a Partner

Four things land on the Process Consultant during onboarding: run the kickoff
call, post an async update afterward, draft the engineering handover
document, and send the post-kickoff NPS form. These interleave with tasks
owned by the Project Manager (confirming the call is scheduled, sending the
client's Slack invite, saving the recording) and the Operations Manager
(financial tracking) — once every onboarding subtask across all three roles
is marked complete, ClickUp automatically moves the partner to **Active**
status.

## Conducting the client kickoff call

This is the client-facing centerpiece of onboarding: rapport, a roadmap
recap, minimum and maximum success criteria, blocking dependencies, and a
close that sets up the onboarding document. Run it in this order.

1. **Set up the call.** Confirm it's being recorded, usually via Fathom, and
   have the onboarding document open and ready to fill out live or right
   after.
2. **Open with introductions and rapport.** Go around both teams. If someone
   new is joining or shadowing, say so plainly — that builds trust instead of
   confusion.
3. **Set the agenda.** State plainly what the call is for and how much time
   is available. If something's been stuck in email or Slack back-and-forth,
   name it up front so it resolves live instead of staying stuck async.
4. **Recap the roadmap.** Share a simple visual of what's done and what's
   next — interviews, surveys, deliverables — and confirm logistics: who's
   the point of contact for what, what's scheduled, what's pending.
5. **Confirm interview logistics.** Number and structure of scheduled
   interviews or surveys, who's included, any review step the client wants
   before materials go to their team. Be explicit about what the client gets
   back — raw data versus a summarized analysis — so expectations are set
   early.
6. **Define minimum success.** Ask stakeholders to complete: *"I would be
   disappointed if we did not at least ___."* Be explicit about scope — this
   phase, or the whole engagement — so answers don't get conflated. There's
   no wrong answer; the goal is calibration, not scoring.
7. **Define maximum success.** *"You would tell everyone about us if we
   achieved ___."* Encourage expansive thinking — this reveals what "wildly
   successful" looks like, beyond the floor from step 6.
8. **Probe and clarify.** Where an answer is vague, pin it down: who does it
   apply to, what does the output actually look like, is this scoped to the
   current phase or the full partnership. Listen for framing concerns too —
   a stakeholder not wanting to overhype the initiative internally — and
   address them directly.
9. **Surface blocking dependencies.** IT/security approval, data access, a
   missing stakeholder — name it plainly as a dependency, restate why it
   matters, and say what happens to the timeline if it isn't resolved.
10. **Clarify end-state expectations.** Who is the final readout presented
    to, in what format, by whom.
11. **Recap next steps and confirm logistics.** Restate what happens in
    sequence, and resolve scheduling conflicts live where you can.
12. **Close, and fill out the onboarding document.** Thank the client,
    confirm any follow-up meetings that came up organically, and capture
    everything — live during the call if possible, immediately after if not.

## Filling out the onboarding document

The onboarding document is the single source of truth about a client's
business, workflows, stakeholders, and goals — it's what saves time during
implementation and keeps the whole team aligned on the same picture. Find it
in ClickUp under **Partner Fulfillment → the partner's folder → Resources →
"Kickoff Document | Partner Name."**

Fill it out as thoroughly as you can, aiming to recreate a clear picture of
the client's world as described on the call:

- Company name and niche
- Departments and stakeholders
- Pain points
- Definition of success
- Company goals
- Company tech stack
- Call schedules
- Notes

Once it's ready for review, drop a message in the relevant ClickUp channel
saying so. This document feeds directly into the Scope of Work, kickoff
decks, definitions of success in ClickUp, and implementation milestone
planning — a thin onboarding doc becomes a scoping problem two weeks later.

## Creating the engineering handover document

Once the kickoff call outcomes are clear, turn that context into a document
engineering can start from without chasing you down.

1. **Gather source material.** The account's signed SOW (scope, deliverables,
   timeline) and the kickoff call recording or transcript.
2. **"Thought vomit" into Claude, same day.** Dump everything relevant in
   whatever order it comes out — what the client cares about, tone and
   personality notes, technical constraints, timeline pressure, anything
   unusual. Don't worry about structure; the point is capturing raw context
   before it's forgotten, not writing a clean document. Waiting a few days
   loses detail that same-day capture wouldn't.
3. **Add the SOW and the kickoff call to the same conversation.** The SOW
   keeps the handover grounded in what was actually contracted — not more,
   not less. The call gives you the client's own language and any nuance
   that didn't make it into the SOW.
4. **Run the Engineering Handover skill** with all three in context, and
   review the output: does it reflect the real scope, in specifics from this
   actual call, not generic templated language?
5. **Post it to the designated ClickUp channel** and tag the relevant
   engineering lead so they're notified it's ready.
6. **Stay available for follow-up.** You have full kickoff context, so
   you're the fastest path to an answer when engineering has a clarifying
   question — don't route it back to the client.

A rushed thought-vomit, or a handover built without the SOW in the loop, is
the most common way scope gets misread before work even starts.

Once the kickoff call, async update, engineering handover, and post-kickoff
NPS form are all done — and the Project Manager's and Operations Manager's
onboarding subtasks are too — the partner moves to Active automatically.
