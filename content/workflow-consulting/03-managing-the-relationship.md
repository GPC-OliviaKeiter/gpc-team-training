# Managing the Relationship

Between the recurring weekly mechanics and closeout, the Process Consultant
carries three ongoing responsibilities: keeping the relationship healthy,
catching scope creep before it becomes a quiet freebie, and proactively
spotting the moments where a partner would benefit from another GPC service.

## Maintaining client relationships (Active stage)

You're the client's single point of contact throughout the Active stage —
not just for status, for sentiment.

- **1-on-1 calls, as needed.** Outside the weekly cadence, schedule one
  whenever there's a relationship, sentiment, or alignment reason to. There's
  no fixed schedule for these.
- **Mid-engagement NPS survey**, sent around the midpoint — distinct from the
  post-engagement feedback form sent at Close Out. This one exists to catch
  and address issues while there's still time to act.
- **Analyze every submission.** Look past the score to what's actually
  driving it — specific praise, complaints, friction points. Bring the team a
  summary with concrete recommendations, not just the raw response forwarded
  along. If the feedback points to a need beyond current scope rather than a
  service gap, that's an upsell signal (see below), not a complaint to
  resolve.
- **Be the point of contact for requirement gathering.** When the team needs
  something from the client, you're the one who asks — not the individual
  engineer or PM. Translate the ask into terms the client understands, and
  relay the answer back clearly and promptly.
- **Follow up before deadlines slip**, not after. Escalate internally if a
  delayed client response is putting the timeline at risk.
- **Own escalations first.** Acknowledge quickly, gather the full picture
  internally, come back with a clear resolution or next step — don't leave
  the client without an update while it's being sorted. If an escalation
  turns out to be an out-of-scope request, route it through scope creep
  below rather than resolving it unilaterally.

## Flagging scope creep in client requests

Client asks that fall outside the current SOW show up on calls, in email, and
over Slack. The job is to catch them, not commit to them on the spot, and
route them to a deliberate team decision.

1. **Spot the request.** Watch for "can you also…", "while you're in
   there…", or anything that would require additional engineering hours
   beyond what's scoped.
2. **Don't commit in the moment** — not even informally, not even "just this
   once." Acknowledge it and say you'll follow up after checking internally.
3. **Flag it internally.** Document what was asked, who asked, which
   channel, and why it looks out of scope. Share it with the Project Manager
   and Operations Manager — and leadership directly if it touches pricing,
   contract terms, or legal exposure.
4. **Let the team weigh in** on feasibility, effort, precedent risk (does
   saying yes once make it the expectation going forward), and relationship
   value. This is a team call, not a solo judgment.
5. **The request resolves one of three ways:** decline, absorb within
   existing scope (minor, with the team's agreement), or escalate to a new
   project proposal — which is also the proactive move covered in Upsell
   Opportunities below. Never start executing on a good-faith assumption
   alone.
6. **Communicate the decision clearly and diplomatically**, and **log the
   outcome** on the partner record — this is what protects against "you did
   this for us before" pushback later.

Being responsive matters, but saying yes to everything erodes margin and
sets a precedent that's hard to walk back. When in doubt, the answer is a
proposal, not a favor.

## Identifying and pitching upsell opportunities

The proactive counterpart to scope creep: spotting where a client would
benefit from a service beyond what's currently scoped, before they've asked
for it.

1. **Know the catalog** — AI Workshop, AI Workflow Assessment, Technical
   Build, AI Adoption Plan, Growth Insurance / Growth Insurance Lite — so a
   need can be matched to the right service.
2. **Watch for signals in touchpoints already built into the engagement:**
   the Discovery/Expansion section of weekly calls, mid-engagement NPS
   feedback that points outside current scope, a pain point repeated across
   multiple stakeholder interviews, goals mentioned at kickoff that fall
   outside the SOW, or a scope-creep request the team decides is big enough
   to need its own SOW. Don't wait for Close Out's post-deployment offer step
   if the moment surfaces earlier.
3. **Validate before pitching.** What is the client actually struggling
   with? Would an additional service meaningfully address it? Is this a
   reasonable time to raise it? If it's ambiguous, loop in the team before
   pitching something half-formed.
4. **Match the signal to a service** — recurring cross-team bottlenecks →
   Workflow Assessment; wanting hands-on build work → Technical Build;
   wanting ongoing coverage → Growth Insurance. If nothing fits cleanly, flag
   it as a potential custom scope rather than forcing it into the wrong
   package.
5. **Pitch it naturally**, tied to what the client actually said or
   experienced — not a generic sales pitch. Be clear about what's included
   and how it builds on the current scope. Don't pressure a same-call
   decision.
6. **Route interest to a formal proposal** and track it so it doesn't fall
   through the cracks after the initial conversation.

Pitching is the Process Consultant's job; pricing, scoping, and the contract
are handled by the broader team once the client is interested.
