# The Sales Process

An opportunity moves **Lead → Qualified Meeting → Discovery → Solution Demo →
Business Considerations → Pricing & Terms → Out for Signature → Closed Won →
Delivery Handoff**, or exits early as Closed Lost or Closed Referred. Not
every opportunity completes every step.

<Callout>The client moves the stage, not GPC finishing an internal task. "We prepared the proposal" doesn't move a deal; "the client is now reviewing it with their CFO" does.</Callout>

## The stages

| Stage | Owner | HubSpot stage | Required before moving here |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead enters GPC | Setter / Lead Owner | n/a | Search email domain → Contact → Company → Deal before creating anything |
| Qualified Meeting | Setter → Closer | n/a | Meets the Qualified Meeting Definition |
| Discovery | Closer | Discovery | Contact, Company, and their deal associations exist; attribution set |
| Qualify and Route | Closer | n/a | Ongoing throughout discovery, not a single checkbox |
| Solution Demo | Closer | Solution Demo | A specific offer identified; prospect has seen or been walked through it |
| Business Considerations | Closer | Business Considerations | Prospect understands the solution and is working the internal approval |
| Pricing & Terms | Closer | Pricing and Terms | Gated: Amount, Expected Go-Live Date, and Project Duration all set |
| Out for Signature | Closer | Out for Signature | The agreement is actually with the client for signature |
| Closed Won | Closer until handoff complete | Closed Won | Signed. A verbal yes is not Closed Won |
| Delivery Handoff | Closer → Delivery | n/a | Sales-to-Delivery handoff complete |

Closed Lost: the opportunity is no longer realistically active (declined, competitor
selected, no path to budget, need disappeared). Closed Referred: GPC intentionally
sends it to another firm instead, then the Referrals pipeline process takes over.
A referral out is not a loss.

<Callout>Amount means total contract value, not the monthly rate. $2,500/month × 6 months is a $15,000 Amount, not $2,500.</Callout>

<Callout>Once a deal reaches Out for Signature, never move it backward. If something changes: leave the stage, document what happened, update the next step. If it dies, close it as Closed Lost.</Callout>

## Choosing the pipeline

Pipeline says what kind of opportunity this is; stage says where the client is in
their buying process. The two answer different questions and shouldn't be mixed.

| Pipeline | Use when |
|---|---|
| Prospects | GPC is selling its own services directly, including inbound referrals from Ruben or Yonathan |
| Referrals | GPC is sending the opportunity to another firm (outbound from GPC) |
| Partnerships | The relationship itself is the opportunity, not a client sale |
| HubSpot Shared Selling | GPC and HubSpot are actively co-selling, not just a client that happens to use HubSpot |

A Ruben or Yonathan inbound referral still goes in Prospects. Referrals is only for
opportunities GPC sends elsewhere.

## Lead ownership: Setter → Closer → Delivery

**Setter** owns the lead before the qualified meeting: confirming fit, searching
HubSpot before creating records, associating Contact/Company/Deal correctly,
booking the meeting, and briefing the Closer. Setter ownership ends once the
prospect attends a qualified meeting; don't keep working it unless the Closer
asks for support.

**Closer** owns the opportunity from the first qualified conversation through
Closed Won and the completed delivery handoff: discovery, qualification, offer
selection, solution conversations, business considerations, pricing, proposals
and SOWs, signature, and CRM accuracy.

<Callout>Closed Won is not the end of the process. A clean handoff to Delivery is. The Closer stays responsible until that handoff is complete.</Callout>

**Delivery** owns fulfillment after the handoff: execution, day-to-day
communication, timelines, scope, client satisfaction.

<Callout>Delivery never independently promises new commercial scope, discounts, contract changes, or free additional work. Route those conversations to the deal's commercial owner.</Callout>

An existing client wanting new work (a Workshop client considering Adoption, a
WFA client considering a Build, added scope, a new department) still gets a
**new HubSpot deal**, every time, even though the company already has history
with GPC. Lead source (Ruben Generated, Pete Generated, Yonathan Generated,
Grant Network) records where the opportunity came from; it doesn't decide who
owns running it; the standard ownership process still applies unless someone
is explicitly assigned otherwise.

## Routing quick reference

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| New company may buy GPC services | Prospects |
| Ruben or Yonathan sends GPC a potential client | Prospects |
| Pete generates a GPC opportunity | Prospects |
| Existing client wants a new engagement | New deal in Prospects |
| GPC sends an opportunity to another firm | Referrals |
| Company wants a strategic partnership | Partnerships |
| GPC and HubSpot are actively co-selling | HubSpot Shared Selling |
| Partner refers a potential client to GPC | Prospects |
| Existing open deal, same engagement | Use the existing deal |
| Existing company, separate engagement | Create a new deal |

## Scheduling and blackout dates

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planning | Dates being explored with a prospect. Nothing committed. |
| Held | SOW out for signature, date range reserved. 48-business-hour timer. |
| Confirmed | Signed and locked. True blackout. |
| Completed | Engagement already happened. Historical record. |
| Cancelled | Hold expired without converting, or a confirmed booking fell through. |

A Workflow Assessment blocks 4 days, all interviews inside a single week; splitting
a WFA across weeks requires a change order. An in-person workshop blocks 3 days
(travel out, workshop, travel back); a consultant can work while traveling but
never runs a workshop or a WFA on a travel day. Kickoff must land within 2 weeks
of the interview block. The 48-hour hold timer counts business days only.

<Callout>Stripe marks an invoice paid instantly, which is the fastest path to convert a Held date to Confirmed inside the 48-hour window. ACH can take longer; account for that when a client pays by ACH.</Callout>
