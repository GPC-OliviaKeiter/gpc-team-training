# Prospect FAQ

The approved answers to the questions prospects actually ask, grouped by
topic. These are stances to hold, not scripts to read verbatim.

## About GPC

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| What does GPC actually do? | Helps business teams (sales, marketing, ops, finance, HR, legal) adopt AI so work actually changes, through four offers: Workshops, Workflow Assessments, Adoption Plans, Technical Builds. |
| Are you an AI training company? | Not only. Training is one part of adoption; GPC also identifies workflows, builds skills and agents, deploys technical solutions, sets governance, and measures impact. |
| Are you tied to one AI model? | No. Model-agnostic; recommend tools based on the business problem, existing tech, and security needs. Don't turn discovery into a "which model is best" debate. |
| Do you train or fine-tune models? | Rarely. Start at the workflow, data, prompt/skill, automation, or integration layer first. Don't sell a complicated technical solution just because they asked for one. |
| Can you integrate with our CRM/ERP/other systems? | Potentially, but never promise compatibility before understanding the platform, data quality, API availability, and security requirements. A named platform starts technical discovery; it isn't permission to promise the integration. |

## Choosing the right engagement

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Where should we start? | From the business problem: inconsistent adoption → Workshop or Adoption; don't know where AI creates value → Workflow Assessment; know what to build → Technical Build; need AI embedded company-wide → Adoption. Discovery determines the door. |
| Do we always need a Workflow Assessment first? | No. Skip it when the client already has strong process documentation and clearly defined, sized opportunities. Don't sell discovery they've already done. |
| We already know the AI projects we want. Why assess anything? | Check whether the workflow, outcome, systems, data dependencies, and owner are already answered. If yes, don't repeat the work; if not, the assessment creates the shared clarity a bigger engagement needs. |

### Workshops

Every Workshop combines discovery, customized training, and partner-specific
recommendations: a 40-question team AI survey, usage data review, audience
analysis, three modules from the catalog, a customized agenda and keynote,
champion identification, and starter resources.

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Silver vs. Gold? | Silver: customized via survey data, usage data, and discovery. Gold: adds stakeholder interviews per breakout track, skeptic/champion mapping, three fully custom breakout packages, 2-3 partner-specific starter AI skills, on-site delivery, and executive dinner. |
| Is Silver still customized? | Yes, not off-the-shelf; module selection, agenda, and keynote are all built from that partner's own data. |
| Can you work with advanced users, not just AI 101? | Yes, the catalog is selected based on maturity and usage data. Confirm any highly specialized/technical request with Delivery before promising it. |
| Can it be split across time zones or a different format? | Don't promise on the call. Capture the requirement, confirm feasibility with Delivery, then communicate the agreed plan. |
| Will it be recorded? | Don't promise a recording as a standard deliverable. Confirm with Delivery first if specifically requested. |
| Why isn't one Workshop enough? | "AI is like going to the gym. You can't do it once and expect the result to stick." A Workshop creates the first wins; sustained adoption needs reinforcement. |

### Workflow Assessments

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| What does it give us? | Where AI and automation create the most business impact: leadership kickoff, stakeholder interviews, AI maturity data, workflow analysis, prioritization, a readout, and leadership handoff. |
| Silver vs. Gold? | Silver, $10,000: under 100 employees, 5 hours of stakeholder interviews, AI Maturity Survey, readout. Gold, $20,000: 100+ employees, 10 hours of interviews, up to 2 workflow demos, everything in Silver. |
| Can we take the assessment and build it ourselves? | Yes. The client owns the work; there's no requirement to continue into a GPC build. |
| Is the assessment just a report? | No, it's a prioritized roadmap. Don't promise a working production build is included; technical implementation is a separate scope unless explicitly sold. |

### Adoption

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| What is an Adoption Plan? | A six-month engagement running Train → Adopt → Deploy → Maintain: group and 1:1 training, production skills and agents, usage/token monitoring, onboarding/offboarding, governance support. |
| Why six months? | Behavior change needs time to build capability, create wins, deploy them, measure, improve, and make habits repeatable. Not a single event. |
| Do you train everyone or just power users? | Usually both. "Everyone to a B+, not a few people to an A." Power users push capability forward; the broader org needs enough fluency that AI isn't dependent on five people. |

### Technical Builds

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Do you build the automations and AI systems? | Yes, when the opportunity needs engineering, integrations, data infrastructure, or agents. Silver: $15,000/month, 15 engineering hours. Gold: $25,000/month, 25 engineering hours. Never promise a timeline or named engineer before the scope is reviewed. |

## Data and AI readiness

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Can we just connect AI to all our company data? | Ask "do you trust the data today?" first. Bad data isn't an AI problem to paper over; it may need fixing before AI layers on top. |
| We're mid-ERP/CRM migration, should we wait? | Not necessarily; some workflows don't depend on the system being replaced. If the migration materially affects the proposed work, escalate before promising scope or pricing. |

## ROI and measurement

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| How do you measure whether AI is working? | Start with the business outcome, not usage: time saved, labor cost, revenue impact, margin, error reduction, cycle time, or progress against an existing KPI. |
| How do you prove ROI before something is built? | Estimate from real operating inputs (frequency, duration, labor cost, error cost, revenue affected). Never invent a number to make the project look attractive. |
| Does AI mean reducing headcount? | No. A multiplication tool, not a subtraction tool; the goal is more output and capability from the people already there. Don't promise a headcount outcome discovery hasn't established. |

## Ownership and long-term dependency

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Who owns what you build? | The client, per the agreement. "I would love to never be re-hired." The goal is leaving them more capable, not proprietary lock-in. |
| What if we want to maintain it ourselves later? | Valid outcome; GPC can train internal owners and document the work so they can run it themselves. Ongoing support should be a choice, not manufactured dependency. |

## Security, privacy, and compliance

<Callout>Never answer a certification or compliance question from memory. Use the current Security/Compliance materials, and if compliance is gating the purchase, escalate to the appropriate security resource.</Callout>

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Are you SOC 2 / HIPAA / FedRAMP compliant? | State the exact current status from current materials. Never guess, minimize the requirement, or promise an unconfirmed certification date. |
| What happens to our data or prompts? | Depends on the AI provider, account type, application, integration, and retention settings. Back the answer with vendor documentation, Trust Center info, and the DPA, not reassurance. |
| Will our data train a model? | No blanket answer; confirm the specific environment and provide the applicable vendor documentation. |

## Licensing and tool decisions

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Which model should we standardize on? | Not a personal preference: weigh existing licenses, adoption, security, ecosystem, use cases, cost, and administration. The goal is a practical operating decision, not a Claude-vs-ChatGPT debate. |
| What does [vendor] cost? | If not verified: "I'll send you the current number today," then verify it. Never guess live vendor pricing. |

## Buying process

| Question | Approved answer |
|---|---|
| Can you send a case study? | Yes, when a relevant one exists. If not, say so; don't stretch an unrelated story to fit. |
| Can you send a proposal? | Yes. Confirm what's being proposed, who reviews it, and when GPC will send it before ending the call, then follow the Post-Call Follow-Up Standards. |
| How soon can we start? | Use current delivery availability. State a real capacity constraint if one exists; don't manufacture scarcity or promise an unverified kickoff date. |
| We don't have budget, is there a smaller option? | Don't discount immediately. Ask "what would have to be true for a budget to exist?" Any non-standard pricing still routes through the approved discount process. |

## When to escalate

<Callout>"I don't want to guess at that. I'll get you the exact answer" is a stronger sales answer than being confidently wrong.</Callout>

Escalate rather than answer or promise beyond your authority: security or compliance as a buying gate, an engineering-only or SDLC assessment, a major ERP or data-platform dependency, an enterprise licensing decision, a PE firm/board/fund-level stakeholder, a nonprofit or non-standard discount, a keynote or major executive event, custom technical architecture you can't validate, another partner's time or deliverable, an unscoped custom build, or anything you're not confident is factually correct.
