# Qualifying a Lead

Qualification isn't about forcing every prospect into a GPC offer. It's determining
whether there's a real business problem, whether GPC is positioned to solve it, and
which offer or partner the opportunity should route to. When something isn't a fit,
**decline the scope, not necessarily the company.**

## BANT

| Field | Track | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Budget | The client's actual stated budget or latest signal | Never record GPC's quote as the client's budget |
| Authority | Who approves, who signs, who influences, buyer vs. internal champion | Append-only, never overwrite previous notes |
| Need | Pain, business problem, desired outcome, impact | Append-only, never overwrite previous notes |
| Timing | The client's buying timeline: Immediate (0-30 days), This Quarter (1-3 months), Next Quarter (3-6 months), 6+ Months/Future, No Timeline/Exploratory | Scheduling availability isn't Timing. "I'm free Thursday" is scheduling; "we need this live before Q4" is Timing |

## The Qualified Meeting Definition

A meeting counts toward Setter KPIs and compensation when every one of these is
true, and the prospect attends:

| Must be true | What it means |
|---|---|
| Represents a real business | An identifiable company that could realistically buy, not a job seeker, recruiter, vendor pitching GPC, or spam contact |
| Relevant to GPC's scope | A reasonable connection to Workshop, Workflow Assessment, Adoption Plan, or Technical Build |
| A business-side buyer or problem | Contact owns or influences a relevant problem, or can reach the people who do; doesn't have to be the final decision-maker |
| A plausible business need | At least one identifiable reason for the call; the need can still be vague, since Discovery diagnoses it |
| Actually attended | A no-show, cancel, or reschedule-without-attending doesn't count as a show |

None of these alone disqualify a meeting: the prospect already uses AI heavily,
doesn't know their budget yet, isn't the economic buyer, is still exploratory, or
can't name the exact solution they need. What's never qualified: a vendor or
recruiter pitching GPC, a purely educational or research request, a duplicate
booking of the same opportunity, or a call with only GPC attendees.

<Callout>A meeting can be correctly qualified at booking and still end in Closed Lost, Closed Referred, or no next step. That doesn't retroactively make it unqualified, unless information available before the call already showed it failed the standard.</Callout>

<Callout>Setter compensation rule: a meeting counts once it meets the criteria above and the prospect attends. It doesn't need to advance stages, get a proposal, include the economic buyer, or close.</Callout>

## Fit signals and typical route

| Signal | Typical route |
|---|---|
| Buyer is a business team (Sales, Marketing, Finance, Ops, HR, Legal, known together as "SMOF") | Engineering-only needs usually route elsewhere |
| AI licenses already active but adoption stalled | Workshop, then Adoption when appropriate |
| A named business goal AI can accelerate | Workflow Assessment |
| Adoption is uneven across the org | Workshop → Adoption |
| Headcount known | Under 100 employees: Silver, $10,000. 100+: Gold, $20,000 |
| Needs to build AI skills org-wide | Workflow Assessment, Workshop, or Adoption Plan depending on whether they need to find opportunities, build capability, or both |

<Callout>Ask about headcount early. Pricing and delivery depend on it, and you can't quote accurately without knowing company size.</Callout>

## Not a fit, or not yet

| Situation | What to say or do |
|---|---|
| Engineering-only scope | "We work with business teams. Engineering assessments are a different scope with a different lead." Route to the appropriate technical partner. |
| Wants model training or fine-tuning | "That isn't our typical starting point... we usually start at the workflow and skill layer, where the answer is often simpler than a custom model." |
| Requires a compliance certification GPC doesn't have | Be direct: name the current posture (e.g. "not SOC 2 certified today, Vanta is underway") and provide current documentation. |
| Wants a keynote with no follow-through | "A keynote with no follow-through isn't typically the type of engagement we build around." Look for a broader Workshop, Adoption, or rollout opportunity first. |
| No business goal named | Ask what existing goal this would make happen faster or bigger. If none, park it until one exists. |
| Budget truly zero with no path | Ask what would have to be true for a budget to exist. If there's truly no path, stop advancing. |

<Callout>Don't disqualify a sophisticated buyer just because they already use AI heavily. It often means their problems are more complex, not absent. Never guess at a live number (pricing, seat costs, vendor fees): say "I'll send you the real number today" and follow up.</Callout>

## Pre-call preparation

10 to 15 minutes for a normal first Discovery call is enough. Spend more only when
the opportunity is strategically important, senior executives are attending, a
partner is involved, the relationship is complex, or it's later-stage.

<Figure id="sales-hubspot-bant-fields" caption="A deal record in HubSpot showing the BANT fields populated" spec="HubSpot, an open deal, BANT custom fields panel visible" />

Before joining: search HubSpot first (email domain → Contact → Company → Deal,
never create a new record without checking); confirm who's attending and whether
the economic buyer appears to be present; know the lead source; review why they
booked; know the company at a basic level (what they do, size, industry); look
for business context (growth, layoffs, acquisitions, new leadership, AI
initiatives); check for existing AI activity; consider what the contact's role
likely cares about; form a hypothesis for the likely entry offer and review that
product's guide; list your open questions; check for known risks (security,
compliance, a migration, a prior lost deal).

<Callout>Don't overprepare. The goal is better questions, not a pre-built solution or a generic pitch deck before you've heard the problem.</Callout>
