# Discovery and Demo

Discovery isn't pitching every GPC service. It's understanding the business,
finding the real problem, and figuring out what needs to happen for the client
to move forward.

## Opening the call

1. **Value their time.** "What makes today's call a win?"
2. **Name the two anti-patterns.** "Two things not to do: send the blanket AI
   license email, or spend thirty days brainstorming AI ideas. Both turn AI
   into a side hobby nobody has invested in."
3. **State the thesis.** "AI is a change management problem, not a technology
   problem. Do you already have the tools?"
4. **Stop talking.** The client does most of the talking from here.

## Discovery question order

| # | Ask | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "We just bought Claude/OpenAI/Gemini, teach our team" or "what are the biggest levers we can pull with AI"? | Adoption problem vs. implementation problem |
| 2 | "What are your business goals for the next two or three quarters?" | Whether the work connects to a real objective |
| 3 | "What does productivity mean here? Minimum and maximum definition of success." | A measurable target, not a vague "save time" |
| 4 | "How is work created, delegated, completed, and marked complete?" | Manual handoffs, bottlenecks, automation opportunities |
| 5 | "Do you trust your reports and your data?" | Whether a data problem has to be solved before the AI problem |
| 6 | "How many people are there, and which department carries the most weight?" | Scope, rollout size, likely starting point |
| 7 | "Who else has to say yes, and who signs?" | Authority: update the field with anything new |
| 8 | "What's on your calendar the next ninety days?" | Timing: a real event can create a deadline; update the field |
| 9 | "Is anything already in flight that could block us? ERP migration, InfoSec review, data project?" | Blockers found now are manageable; found after the SOW, expensive |

## Phrases that land

| When | Say |
|---|---|
| They want a one-time workshop | "AI is like going to the gym. Two or three times a week keeps you in shape. You can't do it once and expect the result to stick." |
| Job loss comes up | "It's a multiplication tool, not a subtraction tool." |
| Only a few people get trained | "You just moved the bottleneck." |
| They ask who should be trained | "Everyone to a B+, not a few people to an A." |
| They already have internal AI leadership | "We're the hands. You're the head." |
| Explaining how adoption spreads | "Win, story, curiosity, next win." |
| Employees are nervous about AI | Pro Dreamer / Pro Complainer: "What's the magic-wand version of your job?" then "What's one thing you'd remove from your job tomorrow?" |
| Discussing the long-term engagement | "I would love to never be re-hired." |

## The last five minutes

Many strong discovery calls are lost here. Before it ends:

- **Price.** Say a number out loud, or a realistic range. Don't let pricing stay
  a mystery until a proposal shows up.
- **Artifact.** State exactly what's being sent and when ("I'll send the
  assessment by Thursday," not "I'll follow up with some information").
- **Next call.** Propose two specific times live ("Tuesday at 2:00 or Wednesday
  at 11:00"), not "I'll send you my calendar."
- **Right people.** Ask who else should be in the room for the next conversation.

<Callout>Use scarcity only when it's real. An actual constraint ("our next kickoff is the week of September 21") builds credibility; manufactured urgency destroys it.</Callout>

## The Solution Demo stage

Show the prospect what GPC would actually do for a company like theirs, tied
directly back to what discovery surfaced, not a generic capabilities deck. Move
here only once a specific offer is identified and the prospect has seen or been
walked through it. They should leave understanding what GPC recommends, why it
fits the problem, what the engagement accomplishes, what the deliverable looks
like, and what happens next.

## Post-call follow-up

| Trigger | Deadline | Send |
|---|---|---|
| Every prospect-facing call | Same day, before logging off | Written recap: what we heard, what we recommend, pricing discussed, agreed next step, dates, committed materials |
| A pricing question you couldn't answer | Same day | The specific number, in writing |
| Case study or reference requested | Within 24h | A relevant one, or an honest note that none applies |
| Security, privacy, or compliance raised | Within 24h | Trust Center, DPA, current security docs, SOC 2 status |
| Proposal, straw man, or SOW requested | Within 24h unless another date was agreed | The document, with the delivery date stated out loud on the call |
| A partner was mentioned (Pete, Ruben, an ERP or DevOps vendor) | Before the prospect follow-up goes out | Their rate if applicable, context, what you'll tell the prospect, confirmed with them first |
| Economic buyer wasn't on the call | Same day | A short, forwardable recap they can use internally |
| Prospect needs a next meeting | Before ending the call | Two specific proposed times, live |
| Material deal info changed | Within 24h | Update HubSpot per CRM Rules; Authority and Need stay append-only, and only from prospect-confirmed information |

<Callout>"We'll get something over to you" isn't a deliverable. Every call ends with what's being sent, when, and the next meeting time.</Callout>
