# Managing the Relationship

Between the recurring weekly mechanics and closeout, the Process Consultant
carries three ongoing responsibilities: keeping the relationship healthy,
catching scope creep before it becomes a quiet freebie, and proactively
spotting where a partner would benefit from another GPC service.

## Maintaining client relationships (Active stage)

You're the client's single point of contact throughout the Active stage,
not just for status, for sentiment.

- **1-on-1 calls, as needed.** Schedule one whenever there's a relationship,
  sentiment, or alignment reason to. No fixed schedule.
- **Mid-engagement NPS survey**, sent around the midpoint. Distinct from the
  post-engagement feedback form sent at Close Out; this one exists to catch
  issues while there's still time to act.
- **Analyze every submission.** Look past the score to what's driving it.
  Bring the team a summary with concrete recommendations, not the raw
  response forwarded along. A pain point beyond current scope is an upsell
  signal (see below), not a complaint to resolve.
- **Own requirement gathering.** When the team needs something from the
  client, you ask, not the individual engineer or PM. Translate the ask,
  relay the answer back clearly and promptly.
- **Follow up before deadlines slip**, not after. Escalate internally if a
  delayed client response puts the timeline at risk.
- **Own escalations first.** Acknowledge quickly, gather the full picture
  internally, come back with a clear resolution or next step. Don't leave
  the client without an update while it's being sorted. An escalation that
  turns out to be an out-of-scope request routes through scope creep below,
  not resolved unilaterally.

## Flagging scope creep in client requests

Client asks that fall outside the current SOW show up on calls, in email,
and over Slack. Catch them, don't commit on the spot, route them to a
deliberate team decision.

1. **Spot the request.** Watch for "can you also," "while you're in
   there," or anything needing engineering hours beyond scope.
2. **Don't commit in the moment**, not even informally. Acknowledge it, say
   you'll follow up after checking internally.
3. **Flag it internally.** Document what was asked, who asked, which
   channel, and why it looks out of scope. Share with the Project Manager
   and Operations Manager, and leadership directly if it touches pricing,
   contract terms, or legal exposure.
4. **Let the team weigh in** on feasibility, precedent risk, and
   relationship value. This is a team call, not a solo judgment.
5. **Resolve it.**

   | Outcome | When |
   |---|---|
   | Decline | The request doesn't fit the engagement |
   | Absorb within existing scope | Minor, with the team's agreement |
   | Escalate to a new proposal | Big enough to need its own SOW (see Upsell Opportunities below) |

6. **Communicate the decision clearly and diplomatically, and log it** on
   the partner record. This is what protects against "you did this for us
   before" pushback later.

<Callout>Never start executing on a good-faith assumption alone. When in doubt, the answer is a proposal, not a favor.</Callout>

## Identifying and pitching upsell opportunities

The proactive counterpart to scope creep: spotting where a client would
benefit from a service beyond current scope, before they've asked for it.

Know the catalog: AI Workshop, AI Workflow Assessment, Technical Build, AI
Adoption Plan, Growth Insurance and Growth Insurance Lite. Watch for signals
already built into the engagement: the Discovery and Expansion section of
weekly calls, mid-engagement NPS feedback pointing outside current scope, a
pain point repeated across stakeholder interviews, goals from kickoff that
fall outside the SOW, or a scope-creep request the team decides needs its
own SOW. Don't wait for Close Out's post-deployment offer if the moment
surfaces earlier.

| Signal | Maps to |
|---|---|
| Recurring cross-team bottlenecks | Workflow Assessment |
| Wants hands-on build work | Technical Build |
| Wants ongoing coverage | Growth Insurance |
| Ambiguous | Flag as potential custom scope, don't force it into the wrong package |

1. **Validate before pitching.** What is the client struggling with? Would
   an additional service meaningfully address it? Is this a reasonable
   time to raise it? Loop in the team if it's ambiguous.
2. **Pitch it naturally**, tied to what the client actually said or
   experienced, not a generic sales pitch. Be clear about what's included
   and how it builds on the current scope. Don't pressure a same-call
   decision.
3. **Route interest to a formal proposal** and track it so it doesn't fall
   through the cracks.

Pitching is the Process Consultant's job. Pricing, scoping, and the
contract belong to the broader team once the client is interested.
