# Scope, Estimates, and Retros

## SOW alignment review

Every 3rd week of the month, per the PM Cadence: pull the active partner
list from the SOW List, cross-reference each partner's signed SOW in Google
Drive (`03 Sales` → `03.2 Proposals and SOWs`), and compare the month's
delivered and in-progress work against what the SOW actually specifies.

Watch for these scope creep signals: a task or request that doesn't map to
any SOW line item; recurring "quick asks" that individually look small but
add up past the monthly hour cap; a request for a different category of
work than what was scoped; hours consistently over cap with no SOW
amendment to match; a one-time deliverable turning into a recurring ask; a
new client-side stakeholder introducing requirements outside the original
engagement.

<Callout>A single small deviation is a judgment call the PM can absorb. A pattern, recurring requests, consistent overages, or a new deliverable category, is scope creep.</Callout>

When it is scope creep: flag it, confirm the finding with the Process
Consultant or Senior AE before raising it with the client, then decide the
path: absorb it as a one-off if minor and non-recurring, negotiate a change
order or SOW amendment if substantial or recurring, or decline and redirect
the client to the proper process. If a change order is approved, update the
SOW record in ClickUp and the signed document in Drive, and tell the
delivery team so their task load and estimates reflect the new scope.

For a SOW nearing its end date or hour cap: confirm whether everything
committed has actually been delivered, then coordinate with the Process
Consultant on renewal, extension, a different engagement type, or formal
closeout, well before the SOW ends.

## Scoping time estimates for hourly retainer tasks

<Callout>This SOP exists in both the Engineering and Project Management handbooks with the same rules; the PM handbook's own Roles & Responsibilities table is currently blank in ClickUp. The roles match Engineering's copy: Senior AE/PM sets and reviews estimates, the executor works inside them.</Callout>

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| The executor never sets their own estimate | Senior AE or PM sets it, based on complexity, technical risk, and the assigned executor's skill level, never how fast they personally could do it |
| Estimate for real conditions | Assume executors may be less experienced and platforms behave inconsistently; never estimate for a perfect-execution scenario |
| Apply the buffer ("the bloating rule") | A 0.25 multiplier: a 1-hour task estimates at about 1.25 hours, a 2-hour task at about 2.5. Controlled padding, not arbitrary inflation |
| Use 15-minute increments only | Round every estimate to 15, 30, 45, 60 minutes, etc.; minimum estimate is 15 minutes |
| Accuracy target | 80-90% of tasks should land inside their scoped estimate; consistent overruns become a tracked improvement area |

## Project retro procedure

**Phase 1, offboarding documentation collection.** When a client enters
closeout, the PM sends the Offboarding Documentation Form to every team
member who worked with the partner; each completes it individually.

**Phase 2, Project Retro Meeting, escalation-driven only.** Management
decides whether one is warranted, reserved for a partner that churned with
issues or escalations, not every closeout. When held, it covers what
happened over the engagement, where it went wrong, how the service could
improve, and what the team did well. Responses get marked complete once
discussed.

**Phase 3, the monthly offboarding review, the regular cadence.** The PM and
Operations Manager meet at month-end to discuss that month's offboarding
responses, look for patterns, and ideate new policies or protocols. Marked
complete once discussed.

<Callout>A dedicated Project Retro Meeting is the exception, only for a churned or escalated partner. Every other closeout runs through the standing monthly PM/OM review.</Callout>

## Recurring client satisfaction tracking

Every 1-2 months for the life of the engagement, the PM reminds the Process
Consultant or Senior AE to send the mid-engagement NPS survey. Once the
client submits it, the PM reviews the response and announces the score in
the partner's ClickUp channel.

If the score is low, the PM informs the Operations Manager and Grant, then
brings proposed ideas for improving the service and increasing satisfaction
on that account.

<Callout>This is the recurring mid-engagement touch. The first, post-kickoff NPS is sent during Onboarding (see Partner Lifecycle: Onboarding to Offboarding).</Callout>
